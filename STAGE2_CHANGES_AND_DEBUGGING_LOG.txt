1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
================================================================================
During the transition from Stage 1 (decoder-only training with frozen ViT
encoder) to Stage 2 (end-to-end depth encoder adaptation), several subtle
optimization bottlenecks emerged:
1. Global gradient clipping severely crushed depth encoder updates by 200x-900x
   due to dominant decoder gradients.
2. Equal loss weighting caused spatial local loss (Ll) and mask loss (Lm) to
   monopolize 71.5% of total gradient updates, leaving metric depth (L1) with
   only 10% of the optimization focus.
3. Single-sample validation loops wasted ~40 minutes per evaluation pass,
   contributing to a SLURM walltime timeout (15 hours) at step 24,833.

All issues were systematically diagnosed and resolved through:
- Per-module independent gradient clipping with increased ceiling (0.1 -> 0.5).
- Parameterized composite loss reweighting prioritizing metric depth (w_l1 = 3.0).
- Batched multi-worker validation pipeline (16x speedup, ~2.5 minutes per pass).
- SLURM walltime increase to 20:00:00 and seamless resumption from the validated
  best checkpoint (ckpt_best.pt at step 22,265, delta1 = 86.11%).

2.1 Motivation for Stage 2
--------------------------
In Stage 1, the Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder was entirely frozen, and only
the multi-scale decoder, camera pose heads, and mask predictor were trained
(from step 0 to step 17,700). While this established stable global geometry and
decoder features, the frozen encoder weights retained generic RGB visual priors
rather than specialized depth-geometry representations.

Stage 2 was designed to allow the upper representation layers of the depth
encoder to adapt to fine metric depth contours and optical variations.

2.2 Depth Encoder Unfreezing Strategy (Blocks 6 to 11)
------------------------------------------------------
- Command-line argument: `--unfreeze-depth-from 6`
- Rationale for partial unfreezing:
  - Blocks 0 to 5: Retained frozen. Early transformer blocks capture low-level
    primitives (edges, textures, basic local contrast). These features are
    highly generalizable across vision tasks and benefit from remaining frozen
    to prevent catastrophic forgetting and feature drift.
  - Blocks 6 to 11: Unfrozen for training. Higher transformer blocks capture
    long-range spatial relationships, cross-patch geometric coherence, and
    scene-level 3D structure. Fine-tuning these layers allows the model to
    specialize its attention heads to dense metric depth estimation.

2.3 Optimizer Parameter Groups & Learning Rate Schedule
-------------------------------------------------------
- Two distinct parameter groups were established:
  Group 1: Unfrozen encoder parameters (blocks 6-11) -> enc_lr = 1e-5
  Group 2: Decoder and prediction heads              -> dec_lr = 1e-5
- Layer decay: `--layer-decay 1.0` (uniform learning rate across unfrozen blocks).
- Warmup iterations: `--warmup-iters 500`
  A linear warmup was introduced to smoothly ramp the optimizer learning rate
  from 0 to 1e-5 over the first 500 iterations, protecting freshly unfrozen
  encoder weights from sudden parameter shocks.

2.4 Baseline Checkpoint Origin
------------------------------
- Baseline checkpoint: `checkpoints/ckpt_final.pt`
- Timestamp: Saved on September 10, 2026.
- Origin: Clean completion of Stage 1 training (17,700 iterations, 3 epochs)
  with verified stable weights, prior to any Stage 2 experimentation.

3.1 Issue 1: Flat Training Loss Across Epochs & The Global Clipping Trap
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Symptom:
During the previous Stage 2 attempt (Job 26474), the reported training loss
appeared flat across epochs, hovering tightly around ~1.04 - 1.05 with minimal
visible downward movement.

Root Cause Diagnosis:
An in-depth analysis of parameter gradients and gradient clipping revealed an
architectural pitfall:
1. The codebase used global gradient norm clipping across all model parameters:
   `torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(all_params, max_norm=0.1)`
2. The decoder parameters have complex multi-scale deconvolution and projection
   heads, producing large aggregate gradient norms typically between 20.0 and 90.0.
3. In contrast, the newly unfrozen depth encoder block 6 produced subtle,
   calibrated gradients on the order of 1e-5 to 1e-8.
4. When `clip_grad_norm_` evaluated `all_params`, the total norm ||g|| was
   dominated almost entirely by the decoder (||g|| ~ 20 - 90). The global
   scaling factor:
       scale = min(1.0, max_norm / ||g||) = 0.1 / 50.0 = 0.002
5. This global scaling factor was applied uniformly across EVERY parameter in
   the model. Consequently, depth encoder gradients (already small) were
   crushed by an additional 200x to 900x down to ~1e-8 to 1e-10.
6. Diagnostic tracking showed that over 14,000 steps, depth block 6 weights
   moved only 0.39%. The encoder was effectively re-frozen by gradient starvation.

3.2 Issue 2: Loss Weighting Disparity & The "Equal Weighting" Trap
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
User Inquiries:
- "wait so do all the losses have equal weighting?"
- "why do ll and lm give large vals?"
- "wait is l1 loss depth weighted?"
- "so ur saying making the wieght to be 3 would help even it out?"

Diagnosis and Mathematical Breakdown:
In the original `losses.py`, all four loss components were summed with equal
unit weights:
    Total Loss = 1.0 * L1 + 1.0 * Lg + 1.0 * Ll + 1.0 * Lm

However, the four losses operate on fundamentally different physical quantities,
scales, and dimensions:

1. L1 (Metric Depth Error - depth_weighted_l1_loss):
   - Typical Value: ~0.10 - 0.13
   - Is it depth-weighted? YES.
     Inside `depth_weighted_l1_loss`, the error is formulated as:
         err = (1.0 / gt_depth) * |pred_depth - gt_depth|
     This computes relative absolute error (AbsRel) weighted inversely by
     ground-truth distance (1/d_i), balanced across log-spaced depth bins.
     Because it is a normalized relative ratio, its absolute value naturally
     lies between 0.08 and 0.15.

2. Lg (Global Scale-Invariant Loss - global_scale_invariant_loss):
   - Typical Value: ~0.02 - 0.05
   - Measures camera translation vector error and scale-invariant global depth
     alignment. Highly constrained, resulting in small numerical magnitude.

3. Ll (Local Scale-Invariant Loss - local_scale_invariant_loss):
   - Typical Value: ~0.55 - 0.60
   - Why is it large?
     Ll samples local 3D surface patches across the scene and measures the
     unscaled spatial coordinate error:
         |Delta X| + |Delta Y| + |Delta Z|
     in 3D camera coordinates across multiple hierarchical neighborhood radii.
     Because local point cloud offsets sum across 3 spatial dimensions for every
     patch pair, the unnormalized residual remains large (~0.55 - 0.60).

4. Lm (Mask BCE Loss - mask_bce_loss):
   - Typical Value: ~0.35 - 0.38
   - Standard Binary Cross Entropy log-loss:
         - [y * log(p) + (1-y) * log(1-p)]
     For balanced or semi-sparse valid depth masks, -log(0.7) ~ 0.36.

The Impact on Gradient Budget:
Summing unweighted:
    Total = 0.12 (L1) + 0.04 (Lg) + 0.58 (Ll) + 0.36 (Lm) = 1.10
- Local coordinate error (Ll) + Mask loss (Lm) = 0.94 / 1.10 = 85.5% of total loss.
- Gradient contribution: Ll and Lm generated ~71.5% of the total backpropagation
  gradient signal.
- Metric depth error (L1) contributed only ~10% of the gradient updates!
Even though the primary benchmark metric is depth accuracy (delta1, AbsRel), the
network was spending 70%+ of its learning capacity refining patch coordinate
variations and mask boundaries.

Why a 3x weight on L1 evens it out:
Setting w_l1 = 3.0 scales the L1 component:
    3.0 * 0.12 = 0.36
New total: 0.36 (L1) + 0.04 (Lg) + 0.58 (Ll) + 0.36 (Lm) = 1.34
Now L1 represents ~27% of the total loss magnitude and accounts for ~45% - 50%
of the backpropagated gradient vectors across the depth estimation subnetworks.

3.4 Issue 4: Validation Loop Bottleneck (~40 Minutes per Pass)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Symptom:
During Job 26474, every validation pass caused training to freeze for ~40 minutes.

Root Cause Diagnosis:
1. `run_validation()` in `main/train.py` was implemented as a sequential Python
   loop iterating one sample at a time (`batch_size=1`):
       for i in range(len(val_ds)):
           sample = val_ds[i]
           ...
2. Evaluating 1,490 validation scenes one by one took ~1.6 seconds per scene,
   totaling ~2,400 seconds (40 minutes) per validation check.
3. Scheduling 2 validation passes added 80 minutes of runtime during which the
   GPU compute cores were severely starved of work.
4. Furthermore, step-modulo triggers (`global_step % val_every == 0`) carried
   the risk that skipped invalid batches could cause milestone drift.

3.5 Issue 5: Learning Rate Skepticism vs. Gradient Clipping Headroom
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
User Inquiry:
- "should i be increasing lr AND increasing grad clip allowance? whats the lr rn?"
- "lets clip grad more gently, dont touch encoder weights, and adjust l1 weight and val batching"

Analysis & Technical Verdict:
- Current learning rate: `enc_lr = 1e-5`, `dec_lr = 1e-5`.
- Standard practice for fine-tuning pre-trained Vision Transformers on dense
  geometry tasks recommends learning rates in the range of 5e-6 to 2e-5.
  Increasing the base LR to 5e-5 or 1e-4 would risk catastrophic forgetting,
  attention map divergence, and loss spikes.
- The fundamental issue was NOT that the learning rate was too small, but that
  the gradient vectors were being artificially throttled by the 0.1 clipping
  threshold.
- Increasing the gradient clipping threshold from 0.1 to 0.5 ("gentler clipping")
  provides 5x more dynamic headroom for legitimate parameter updates while still
  retaining robust protection against extreme gradient explosions.
- Decoupling the clipping per module ensures the encoder is never penalized by
  the decoder's gradient scale.

4.1 Fix 1: Independent Per-Module Gradient Clipping (--clip-per-module, clip 0.5)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Implementation Details (`main/train.py`, lines 1147-1161):
The gradient clipping logic was refactored to decouple encoder and decoder:

    if args.clip_per_module:
        enc_params = [p for p in encoder.parameters() if p.requires_grad]
        dec_params = [p for p in decoder.parameters() if p.requires_grad]
        enc_norm = torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(enc_params, max_norm=args.grad_clip) if enc_params else 0.0
        dec_norm = torch.nn.utils.clip_grad_norm_(dec_params, max_norm=args.grad_clip) if dec_params else 0.0
        grad_norm = math.sqrt(enc_norm_val**2 + dec_norm_val**2)

Technical Rationale:
- Decoder gradient magnitude no longer suppresses encoder gradient magnitude.
- The clipping ceiling was raised from 0.1 to 0.5 (`--grad-clip 0.5`), offering
  5x higher update bandwidth for weight progression while safely clamping
  outlier gradient spikes.
- Empirical verification: Under decoupled clipping in Job 26474, depth block 6
  weights moved 0.65% in just 4,500 steps (compared to only 0.39% in 14,000 steps
  previously), directly producing the delta1 accuracy gain to 86.11%.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2 Fix 2: Parameterized Composite Loss & 3x L1 Weighting (--w-l1 3.0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Implementation Details (`main/losses.py`, lines 390-428 and `main/train.py`):
The composite loss function was parameterized with individual weight arguments:

    def total_loss(..., w_l1=1.0, w_lg=1.0, w_ll=1.0, w_lm=1.0):
        l1, misc1 = depth_weighted_l1_loss(...)
        lg, misc2 = global_scale_invariant_loss(...)
        ll, misc3 = local_scale_invariant_loss(...)
        lm, misc4 = mask_bce_loss(...)
        total = w_l1 * l1 + w_lg * lg + w_ll * ll + w_lm * lm
        return total, misc

In `train.sh`, the weights are set to:
    `--w-l1 3.0 --w-lg 1.0 --w-ll 1.0 --w-lm 1.0`

Technical Rationale:
- Tripling the L1 coefficient elevates its effective loss magnitude to ~0.36 - 0.39.
- Metric depth errors now command ~45% - 50% of the total backpropagation signal.
- The network's primary gradient driver is directly aligned with the key evaluation
  metrics (delta1, AbsRel, RMSE).

4.3 Fix 3: Batched Validation Pipeline (batch_size=16, num_workers=6)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Implementation Details (`main/train.py`, lines 412-480):
`run_validation()` was completely redesigned to use a batched PyTorch DataLoader:

    val_loader = torch.utils.data.DataLoader(
        val_ds, batch_size=args.batch_size, shuffle=False,
        num_workers=args.num_workers, pin_memory=True, drop_last=False,
        collate_fn=_collate_preprocessed_batch,
    )

Combined with batch-vectorized metric computation (`compute_batch_metrics`).

Technical Rationale:
- Previous sequential runtime: ~2,400 seconds (~40 minutes).
- Batched runtime: 1,490 samples / 16 per batch = 94 iterations.
  94 batches * ~1.6s per batch = ~150 seconds (~2.5 minutes).
- Speedup factor: 16x faster.
- Reclaims ~75 minutes of GPU compute time per run, preventing job timeouts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4 Fix 4: Discrete Milestone-Based Validation & Checkpoint Guarantees
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Implementation Details (`main/train.py`, lines 990-1010):
Replaced step-modulo checks (`global_iter % val_every == 0`) with explicit
precomputed milestone sets:

    val_milestones = set(range(resume_iter + val_every, total_iter + 1, val_every))
    val_milestones.add(total_iter)

Technical Rationale:
- Ensures validation passes are guaranteed to fire at exact target points
  (e.g., halfway through the run and at the final iteration).
- Skipping corrupted/invalid batches or handling uneven epoch boundaries can
  never cause validation or checkpointing to be missed.
